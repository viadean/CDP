> Chaotic systems like the Lorenz Attractor exhibit a unique physical behavior where the overall volume of a set of starting conditions shrinks over time while individual trajectories within that volume simultaneously pull apart. This phenomenon is driven by a combination of negative divergence, which causes the total space to collapse, and a positive Lyapunov exponent, which forces points to stretch rapidly in a specific direction. In a visual simulation, a tight sphere of particles quickly transforms into a thin, elongated ellipsoid, demonstrating that even as the total volume contracts to a tiny fraction of its original size, the distance between neighboring points increases. This dual mechanism allows the system to generate entropy and scramble local information while still maintaining a stable, bounded global structure.
